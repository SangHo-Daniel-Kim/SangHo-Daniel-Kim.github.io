SangHo Daniel Kim

Hi y’all! I’m SangHo.

I am a visiting student at UT Austin CS, advised by Professors Kristen Grauman and Georgios Pavlakos.

My research interests lie in Computer Vision and Multimodal Learning, with a focus on modeling everyday human activities. In particular, I use modern 3D/4D reconstruction methods to model and analyze interactions to better understand human behaviors.

I am a senior in my undergraduate studies in Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence, and International Relations (3 majors!) at Sungkyunkwan University, and spent a wonderful research year with Professors Sangmin Lee, Jaepil Heo at Sungkyunkwan University, and Hyojin Sung at Seoul National University.

Outside research, I love to serve the public.

I worked as an AI policy advisor for South Korea’s Ministry of Science and ICT, participated as one of 15 Korean youth delegates by the U.S. Department of State, and completed two years of military service in the Air Force Operations Command alongside the U.S. 7th Air Force.

Publications

  1. Personal Visual Context Learning in Large Multimodal Models
    Zihui Xue, Ami Baid, Sangho Kim, Mi Luo, and Kristen Grauman
    arXiv, 2026 [paper] [webpage]

    We introduce Personal Visual Context Learning (Personal VCL), a capability enabling Large Multimodal Models to reason over visual information unique to the user when integrated into wearable devices like smart glasses. We present Personal-VCL-Bench, a benchmark with 2,255 context-query instances across 7 tasks examining three dimensions of a user’s visual world: persons, objects, and behavior. Testing seven frontier LMMs reveals a modality paradox (language summaries outperform raw pixels) and a scaling paradox (more historical context frequently fails to improve accuracy). To address these gaps, we propose the Agentic Context Bank, a model-agnostic baseline that organizes visual history into structured, evidence-linked memory and selectively grounds only relevant entries at inference time.
